Abstract

Knowledge Graph Question Answering (KGQA) over Large Language Models (LLMs) increasingly translates natural-language questions into SPARQL, yet most systems are statically optimized for a single benchmark and degrade abruptly under cross-domain transfer or schema evolution—a recurring obstacle for industrial Knowledge Graphs whose schemas continuously evolve. We present a KG-agnostic Text2SPARQL architecture that operates without task-specific fine-tuning. A meta-controller orchestrates a ReAct agent and an execution-validated, schema-guided fallback pipeline; both paths are grounded by a runtime FAISS-indexed Schema-Discovery service that absorbs structural change without offline retraining. The system is evaluated across four heterogeneous KGs: the open-domain DBpedia, a structurally complex Corporate dataset, and two versions of a proprietary railway metadata KG (TrackVideo, German: Streckenvideo), whose v1→v2 transition exhibits a Mapping Ratio of 0.255 and a normalized graph edit distance of 0.750, representing radical schema refactoring. The hybrid architecture attains a mean F1 of 47.6% with the lowest cross-domain standard deviation (2.33) among compared state-of-the-art systems and preserves performance between TrackVideo v1 (48.0%) and v2 (48.6%) despite the drift. A multi-model Quorum extension over heterogeneous LLM families further increases reliability, with absolute F1 gains of up to +7.0%. The findings support the HARP paradigm—hybrid agentic retrieval with execution-validated routing—for portable, schema-drift-resilient KGQA in production-grade settings.
