Abstract

Legacy mainframe systems support critical financial operations in many large institutions. However, their internal logic, spanning decades of COBOL programs, batch job chains, flat files, and data flows, is rarely documented in a form accessible to new employees. When key engineers leave, institutional knowledge leaves with them — and in regulated industries, that gap is also a compliance exposure: frameworks such as BCBS 239 require banks to understand and demonstrate how critical risk and reporting data flows end-to-end. This talk presents how DIM (Data Insights Monitor), an agentic AI platform developed at Deloitte, was applied at a large bank in the Netherlands to make its legacy batch processing system queryable by anyone and its lineage demonstrable to regulators. The structure of the legacy system — its jobs, procs, steps, programs, applications, data groups and data elements — was modeled as a knowledge graph. DIM sits on top of it, translating natural language questions into graph queries and returning answers as text, tables, or interactive lineage diagrams. A business analyst can ask “show me the data lineage path between these two jobs” or “which programs depend on this data item?” without writing a single line of code. Beyond query translation, the platform brings together two distinct bodies of knowledge — the system’s code, modeled as a knowledge graph, and its surrounding documentation (domain glossaries, operations notes, change records) — through two complementary retrieval modes: graph RAG over the structured lineage and vector RAG over the documentation corpus. A guide library encoding domain rules and terminology steers interpretation, and a self-learning pipeline captures successful patterns from each interaction and feeds them back for future use. A model routing layer selects the appropriate LLM tier per question — fast for lookups, reasoning for impact analysis — keeping cost proportional to task complexity.
