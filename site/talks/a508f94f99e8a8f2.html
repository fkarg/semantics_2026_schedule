Start with the User, Arrive at the Ontology. Six Production Deployments, and the Road Back to RDF [SP]

Jeroen Wouters (Inuits)

13:00 – 14:40 CEST · Kraakhuis

Session time; individual talk times are not specified in the timetable.

Chair: Heiko Paulheim

Online room

Abstract

We were not semantic-data experts. That is not an apology; it is the reason this platform exists.

In the early 2010s we built the City of Antwerp's heritage DAMS on Drupal — common practice then, when stretching a CMS was the pragmatic shortcut for cultural-sector projects. By 2020 the cracks were visible: millions of assets, multiplied integrations, and a record-centric core holding back exactly the features users were asking for. A European research project with several Ghent museums gave us the rarest thing in this sector: permission to rebuild it the way we would build it today.

We did the thing you are not supposed to do at a semantics conference: we did not start from the ontology. The model was expressed in GraphQL and shaped by what cataloguers actually need to see. Our first version modelled the full LDES traversal in the schema and promptly hit the classic n+1 problem — and the fix became the product: each entity declares its own metadata interface and its own way of being fetched, from a REST call down to papers in someone's drawer, with a thin collection service stitching the rest together.

That backbone now runs six deployments with nothing in common: Antwerp's heritage collection, a police sensor network, a marine-science archive where a communications officer tags species from an ontology without ever seeing it, Flanders' central library catalogue, a performing-arts platform, and a corpus of ancient inscriptions at AlUla. LDES in; JSON-LD and IIIF out; open source throughout.

This talk closes where the journey arrives: a proof of concept in which the model is defined in RDF and everything else — including the GraphQL contract — is derived. Start with the user, and you arrive at the ontology through the other door. Afterwards, come and draw your collection at our booth.
