SHACL, The Enabler For Human-Supported, LLM-Based Extraction With High Quality In The Engineering Legal Domain

Achim Reiz, Gregor Seifer, Heinrich Terborg, Claudia Rachadell and León Martinez

13:00 – 14:40 CEST · Concertzaal

Session time; individual talk times are not specified in the timetable.

Chair: Kathrin Füllenbach

Online room

Abstract

In the energy market, legally binding contracts between constructors and operators of a power plant define expectations regarding how a given plant should perform. If these guarantees are not met, the disadvantaged party can claim damages, the calculation of which is also specified within the contract document. Today, these contract documents consist mostly of unstructured text. As a result, calculating potential damages is labor-intensive. With graphs, we aim to transform these contracts into machine-readable documents and perform such checks automatically. Extracting the information with the correct set of terms is challenging: on the one hand, the diverse prose of legal texts requires LLM-based background knowledge for proper interpretation, as the documents do not follow strict guidelines regarding their content and structure. On the other hand, LLMs tend to hallucinate when dealing with vague or underspecified requirements. In the presented use case, we demonstrate how we utilize the Shape Constraint Language (SHACL) to build self-repairing LLM extraction pipelines for generating graphs from unstructured documents and enabling automatic data validation. While the ontology provides the context for what to extract, an integrated validation engine performs checks and delivers targeted repair messages to the LLM whenever the data does not conform to the desired constraints. That allows for a self-repairing data pipeline. Subsequently, we use a SHACL-defined UI to create a data interface for reviewing and editing accessible to legal scholars for a human in the loop architecture.
