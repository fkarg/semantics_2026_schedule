Thursday · 17 September · Industry and Use Cases

From MARC21 to WEMI Without a Library-Software Vendor: A Semantic-Platform Bet

Jeroen Wouters

10:30 – 12:00 CEST · Kabinet

Session time; individual talk times are not specified in the timetable.

Chair: Milan Dojchinovski

Online room

Abstract

Cultuurconnect runs the central catalogue that 300 Flemish public libraries use to catalogue their holdings, with around 60 cataloguers working in it on a typical day. The data sits in MARC21, the legacy library exchange format, even though IFLA's WEMI/LRM model has been the agreed direction for years and most library-software vendors are still slow to ship against it. In 2024 Cultuurconnect concluded that waiting for the next vendor release was not going to get them where they needed to be, and looked across the office floor in Ghent, where Inuits had been building a generic semantic platform called Elody. A joint proof of concept that summer turned into a full build a year later; the new system goes into production in June 2026. Our pitch was contrarian: do not look for a library-software vendor that has yet to ship WEMI; pair a semantic-platform team with the customer's own domain expertise. The catalogue is now an entity-relation model with a thin collection service that distributes data to and from external sources (VIAF, Boekenbank, Muziekweb), with a real-time MARC21 stream kept alive for downstream consumers. The model holds roughly 3 million WEMI entities, is already published as Linked Open Data, and is shaped by cataloguers through ongoing sprint feedback — without compromising the WEMI model we were aiming for. The lesson we did not expect when we started: sector expertise is not the limiting factor in this kind of project. A serious technology partner paired with the customer's own domain people consistently beats a sector-specific software vendor that hasn't yet implemented the new standard.
