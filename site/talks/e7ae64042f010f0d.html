Abstract

Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation (GraphRAG) enhances Large Language Models (LLMs) by integrating knowledge graphs for structured retrieval and reasoning. However, the design space of GraphRAG systems is vast, and systematic guidance on optimal configurations remains scarce. In this paper, we present a comprehensive empirical study identifying best practices across the three core stages of the GraphRAG pipeline: graph-based indexing, graph-guided retrieval, and graph-enhanced generation. Through extensive experiments on multiple datasets (MSMARCO, HotpotQA, and a domain-specific legal corpus), we evaluate key design decisions, including chunking strategies, embedding model selection, prompt engineering for triplet extraction, entity resolution, retriever architectures, traversal depths, reranking models, and graph representation formats for LLM prompts. Our results show that hierarchical chunking at 512 tokens, schema-based extraction prompts, a combined Hypothetical Question and Entities retriever with cross-encoder reranking, and code-like graph representations (GraphML) yield robust performance across domains. We apply the identified best practices to a real-world use case within EU banking regulations, constructing a knowledge graph from the Capital Requirements Regulation (CRR) and the Capital Requirements Directive (CRD IV). A comparative evaluation demonstrates that the optimised GraphRAG system consistently outperforms Naive RAG, HyDE RAG, and Hybrid RAG, achieving overall win rates of 60.6%, 58.0%, and 67.4%, respectively, as judged by an LLM evaluator across comprehensiveness, diversity, empowerment, and correctness metrics. Our findings provide actionable guidelines for deploying GraphRAG in domain-specific, privacy-sensitive settings using open-source models.
