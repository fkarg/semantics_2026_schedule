Using gUFO patterns to create ontologies: unraveling the nature of employment contracts

Kees Nieuwstad and Han Joosten

10:30 – 12:00 CEST · Concertzaal

Session time; individual talk times are not specified in the timetable.

Chair: Harshvardhan Pandit

Online room

Abstract

The Dutch Police need a set of integrated domain ontologies to support their operations and information management. To achieve this goal, they initially adopted DOLCE (implemented in OWL) as an upper ontology to ground domain ontology development. However, DOLCE did not offer modeling patterns to ensure consistency and communication among modelers and stakeholders. The team ended up having different conventions and design decisions that harmed the goal achievement. To overcome these challenges, the police team with our team joined forces to adopt gUFO as the backbone of the ontology development for this project. gUFO is a lightweight OWL implementation of the Unified Foundational Ontology (UFO) that consists of several ontology design patterns that solve common modeling problems in ontology engineering. When our teams worked with these patterns, we noticed that we obtained better communication and higher-quality models. To streamline the application of gUFO patterns, we use OWL reasoning and implemented SPARQL queries that automatically validate ontology design choices continuously for all developers involved in the process. Therefore, we created a collaborative environment that ensures certain ontology quality metrics, following gUFO patterns. One of these patterns allows us to represent different types of employment contracts faithfully. For example, in gUFO, a relator is a relationship treated as a thing in its own right. Instead of just saying "John works for the Police", "John’s contract" is created as a concrete entity that can have its own properties like a start date or legal obligations. In this case, the relator connects an employee to the Police organization. We do not only adopt the pattern but also enforce it via an automated validation pipeline, quickly detecting errors.
