Privacy-Preserving Sharing of Knowledge Graphs at the Bank of Italy

Luigi Bellomarini, Costanza Catalano, Andrea Coletta and Michela Iezzi

10:30 – 12:00 CEST · Kraakhuis

Session time; individual talk times are not specified in the timetable.

Chair: Beatriz Gonçalves Crisóstomo Esteves

Online room

Abstract

Corporate ownership graphs are a strategic knowledge asset for banking supervision and financial intelligence, but their dissemination is constrained by strict confidentiality requirements. This talk presents an industrial case study from the Central Bank of Italy on privacy-preserving sharing of corporate ownership Knowledge Graphs (KGs). In our setting, the KG is not only an extensional graph of asserted ownership triples, but a semantic knowledge base whose intensional layer is specified by Datalog rules. These rules define entailment over the graph and derive supervisory notions such as corporate control, ultimate control, joint control, integrated ownership, and qualifying holdings. As a result, what must be protected is not only the explicit graph, but the combination of asserted data and rule-based inferences. This requirement is central in institutional data-sharing scenarios involving external analysts, researchers, and supervisory partners. Existing anonymization and graph-sanitization techniques typically focus on structural disclosure in the released graph, while they do not address confidential information that may be reconstructed through the semantic rule layer. This gap is relevant as KGs become core assets for analytics and grounding layers for LLM-based applications in high-confidentiality domains. We introduce a semantic anonymization framework for corporate ownership KGs. The method transforms the graph through controlled node and edge additions, entity relabeling, and weight perturbation, while taking the Datalog program into account to prevent both direct disclosure and inference-based leakage. We validate the framework on the Central Bank of Italy ownership dataset and on external benchmarks, assessing formal privacy protection, preservation of supervisory entailments, structural distortion, and downstream analytical utility. The experiments show that the anonymized KGs retain the semantic content required for regulatory analysis while preventing the disclosure of confidential information.
