Abstract

Building Automation and Management Systems offer centralized control and monitoring by integrating smart devices into the environment. Together, these provide building owners and stakeholders with data about energy consumption and other relevant characteristics which can, among others, be used for decision making and the automation of actions. However, due to the proliferation of different protocols and ecosystems, it often remains challenging to seamlessly aggregate and analyse the data streams provided by the connected devices. To overcome these problems, we introduce a knowledge-driven data exchange and validation framework, built upon Semantic Web technologies, that a) facilitates the transparent flow of graph data between smart devices, b) validates these data and generates reports on detected anomalous behaviour in real-time, and c) aggregates and displays the data and any derived insight on dashboards and other interactive web-accessible interfaces. Our framework consists of two core components: the Knowledge Engine, which acts as an information broker and translation layer between device-specific languages and RDF, and the Knowledge Validator, which continuously monitors these RDF streams and reports on deviations from nominal behaviour. We evaluate our framework via requirement-based testing and demonstrate its functionality in a real-world case study in the smart buildings domain, showing that it can facilitate seamless communication between devices of different make and model and that it can help building owners recognize abnormal device behaviour.
