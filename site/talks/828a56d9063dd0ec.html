Abstract

The Interinstitutional Style Guide (ISG) is the primary reference for drafting, editing, translating and publishing documents across the European Union institutions. While the guide contains a large body of editorial, linguistic, legal and publication knowledge, its size and complexity can make the retrieval of relevant information challenging, particularly for users unfamiliar with its structure. To address this challenge, we developed ISGBot, an agentic knowledge framework that combines multimodal retrieval, semantic technologies, reference data and Large Language Models to provide conversational access to the content of the ISG. ISGBot integrates information from PDF documents, extracted text, tables, images, captions, metadata and structured semantic resources represented in RDF and SKOS. A multimodal semantic representation layer combines text embeddings, image embeddings and semantic relations, while concepts identified within the content are linked to authoritative reference data and controlled vocabularies from EU Vocabularies. The retrieval architecture combines BM25, dense vector retrieval using Sentence-Transformers and FAISS, cross-encoder reranking, and RDF/SKOS graph navigation. Unlike traditional Retrieval-Augmented Generation approaches, ISGBot employs an agentic retrieval loop in which Large Language Models assess context sufficiency, reformulate queries, consolidate evidence and generate grounded responses. The prototype was developed and validated in close collaboration with the ISG team of the Publications Office of the European Union, whose feedback was used to evaluate and refine the system. The resulting framework demonstrates how multimodal retrieval, semantic technologies and agentic AI can be combined to provide evidence-based access to complex organisational knowledge resources.
